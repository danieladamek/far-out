## What it is

A federal contracting number has an address, and usually more than one. Statute comes first: the small business goals and the reserved band are in 15 U.S.C. 644, SBIR and STTR in 15 U.S.C. 638, and the prototype other transaction in 10 U.S.C. 4022 [374] [3] [1028]. Regulation implements statute: the Federal Acquisition Regulation, whose pages on acquisition.gov show the circular they are current to (FAC 2026-01, effective 03/13/2026), and SBA's rules in Title 13 of the Code of Federal Regulations [418] [331]. Below both sit policy directives, class deviations and agency pages [5] [400] [310].

A number changes by a dated document. The simplified acquisition threshold went from $250,000 to $350,000, and the micro-purchase threshold from $10,000 to $15,000, by a final rule in the Federal Register (90 FR 41872) effective October 1, 2025 [340] [417]. The same rule moved the subcontracting-plan threshold from $750,000 to $900,000 [340].

The FAR itself now exists in two numberings. Executive Order 14275 of April 15, 2025 ordered a rewrite, and agencies adopt the rewritten model text part by part through class deviations: for Part 19, GSA from November 3, 2025 and DoD from February 1, 2026 [386] [400] [402]. The codified text stays in force beside it. The rule of two is at 19.502-2 in the codified FAR and at 19.104-1 in the model text [384] [391]. The first proposed rules to codify the rewrite appeared on June 23, 2026 [403].

The layers do not update together, so pages disagree. SBA's 13 CFR 125.3 still prints $750,000 for the subcontracting-plan threshold [704]. The codified FAR allows an 8(a) sole-source award up to $8.5 million for manufacturing codes and $5.5 million otherwise, while 13 CFR 124.506 prints $7,000,000 and $4,500,000 and SBA's program page gave $7 million and $4.5 million [338] [331] [310]. The SBIR statute was amended on April 13, 2026, and SBIR.gov still posts the May 2023 directive [1] [7]. The defense statute on commercial solutions openings dropped the word "innovative" on December 18, 2025; DFARS Subpart 212.70 still uses it [1033] [806] [1040].

## The key idea in one picture

Draw a stack of four layers, top to bottom: *statute*, *regulation*, *directive or deviation*, *agency page*. Each layer carries a date stamp. One figure is threaded down through the stack as a vertical line: the subcontracting-plan threshold reads $900,000 in the Federal Register rule and in the FAR, and the line breaks at 13 CFR 125.3, which reads $750,000 [340] [692] [704]. A second thread does the same for the 8(a) sole-source ceiling, breaking between the FAR and 13 CFR 124.506 [338] [331].

To the right, the regulation layer splits into two parallel bars, *codified FAR* and *model deviation text*, joined by a bracket labelled *same rule, two numbers*: 19.502-2 and 19.104-1 [384] [391]. A small tag on the bracket lists which agencies adopted the model text for Part 19, and when [400] [402].

## How this guide uses it

Each threshold in this guide can be traced the same way. The codified FAR page gives the figure and the circular it is current to [418]. The Federal Register rule that last changed it gives the effective date [340]. The overhaul's deviation guide gives the model text and its issue date; it shows Part 1 issued May 2, 2025 and updated March 16, 2026 [389]. Agency pages come last, because they can print older figures [310].

Where two official texts differ, the record prints both under *conflicts* and neither is smoothed away. Every *pending change* carries a status word: proposed, announced, class-deviation, final or pending-implementation. Chapter 8 lists what moved in 2025 and 2026, and chapter 9 explains how the sources were read.

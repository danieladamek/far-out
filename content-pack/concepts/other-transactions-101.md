## What it is

An other transaction (OT) is a legally binding instrument that is not a standard procurement contract, grant or cooperative agreement [1053]. The FAR does not automatically apply to it; the Procurement Integrity Act and fiscal law still do [1053]. DARPA's training lists what falls away: the Competition in Contracting Act, the Truth in Negotiations Act, Cost Accounting Standards, the Contract Disputes Act, the protest process, and the FAR and DFARS. Criminal law and export control remain [1063]. Intellectual property follows the agreement: "Rights need to be specifically stated in the agreement." [1064]

DoD's guide names three types [1049]. A research OT (10 U.S.C. 4021) funds basic, applied and advanced research, expects the other parties to match Government funds where practicable, and carries no authority for follow-on production [1031] [1055]. A prototype OT (10 U.S.C. 4022) needs one of four conditions: a nontraditional defense contractor or nonprofit research institution participating to a significant extent; all significant participants being small businesses or nontraditional defense contractors; at least one third of the cost paid from non-Federal sources; or a written finding of exceptional circumstances [1028]. A production OT follows a prototype project that was competitively awarded and successfully completed, and may go to its participants without further competition [1029].

A commercial solutions opening (CSO) is a general solicitation that "may be structured as prototype OTs pursuant to 10 U.S.C. 4022, or as FAR-based contracts or agreements pursuant to 10 U.S.C. 3458/DFARS 212.70" [1058]. A broad agency announcement (BAA) covers basic and applied research not tied to a specific system, and DoD's comparison page says it "Cannot be used for production" [271] [1057]. The CSO statute dropped its "innovative" limit on December 18, 2025; DFARS Subpart 212.70 has not [1033] [806] [1040].

DoD awards a base agreement to a consortium management organization and selects the member that will do the work; the manager issues a project sub-award to that member, and the base agreement is the starting point for negotiating it [1119]. GAO reports that "Consortia-based OTAs account for half of DOD's prototype OTA obligations from fiscal years 2021 to 2024" [1121]. For most consortia, members pay dues and gain exclusive access to the Government's opportunities [1119].

## The key idea in one picture

Draw a fork that reads left to right. Three doors stand on the left: *CSO*, *consortium request* and *BAA* [1058] [1119] [271]. The CSO door has a side exit, *FAR-based contract* [1058]. The first two doors lead to a central box, *prototype OT*, behind a gate with four leaves: a nontraditional or nonprofit participant, all participants small or nontraditional, one-third cost share, exceptional circumstances. Any one leaf opens it [1028].

From the prototype box an arrow labelled *competitively awarded and successfully completed* runs right to *follow-on production* [1029]. The BAA door leads to a *research* box marked *no production*; the research OT sits there too, with no arrow onward [1057] [1055]. Inside the consortium door sits a short chain: Government, base agreement, manager, project agreement, member [1119]. A sidebar lists what does not apply [1063].

## The maths, gently

$$ P \ge \tfrac{1}{3}\,T $$

- *T* is the total cost of the prototype project and *P* is the part paid from sources other than the Federal Government; this is the third of the four conditions [1028].
- Worked, with illustrative figures: on a project with a total cost of $3,000,000, the condition is met when at least $1,000,000 comes from non-Federal sources [1028].
- The share is one alternative among four, so a project that meets the first or second condition needs no cost share [1028].

## How this guide uses it

The route records for the prototype OT, the research OT, follow-on production, the defense commercial solutions opening, the broad agency announcement and consortium membership open from this picture, as do the civilian OT routes. The OT gates cover approvals, intellectual property, protests, disputes, cost share and the consortium project agreement.

## What it is

Taken together, the FAR's parts describe a sequence without stating it in one place: plan, forecast, research the market, publish a notice, solicit, evaluate, award, debrief [1323] [1324] [562] [595] [599]. <!-- synthesis -->
Planning comes first: "Agencies shall perform acquisition planning and conduct market research for all acquisitions." [1323] Agencies with more than $50 million in annual contract actions prepare a forecast of expected opportunities, and GSA's governmentwide forecast tool needs no registration to search [579] [582]. Market research then tests who could do the work, and a sources-sought notice is, in GSA's words, "primarily used to identify small businesses capable of performing/providing the requirement" [1324] [572].

The purchase then goes public. A proposed action over $25,000 is announced, or synopsized, at SAM.gov, the governmentwide point of entry [562] [418]. Outside commercial buys the notice appears at least 15 days before the solicitation, and above the simplified acquisition threshold offerors get at least 30 days to respond [563]. The method depends on the purchase: Part 13 supplies simplified procedures up to that threshold, Part 12 applies when commercial products or services meet the need, and Part 15 governs negotiated buys [438] [442] [1325]. A Part 15 solicitation states the requirement and the evaluation factors with their relative importance, and a proposal received after the deadline is late [595].

Evaluation always includes price or cost, and a firm with no record "may not be evaluated favorably or unfavorably on past performance" [596]. A firm is registered in SAM when it offers and again at award [303]. An unsuccessful offeror may ask in writing for a debriefing within 3 days of the award notice; the debriefing covers the significant weaknesses or deficiencies in its proposal and does not compare offers point by point [599].

Several people act along the way. The contracting officer decides whether the purchase is set aside and designates the industry code that fixes the size standard [384] [285]. The agency's small business specialist is consulted no later than 30 days before a solicitation, and SBA's procurement center representative recommends set-asides within 15 days of receiving an acquisition package [428] [430]. Once the solicitation is out, "the contracting officer must be the focal point of any exchange with potential offerors" [590].

## The key idea in one picture

Draw a horizontal timeline with nine stations: plan, forecast, market research, sources sought, synopsis, solicitation, evaluation, award, debrief. Under each station sits the FAR part that governs it: Part 7 under plan, Part 10 under market research, Part 5 under synopsis, Parts 12, 13 or 15 under solicitation and evaluation, and Part 19 running the full length as the small-business thread [1323] [1324] [562] [442] [438] [1325] [384].

Two gaps carry their minimum lengths: 15 days from notice to solicitation, and 30 days from solicitation to offers above the simplified acquisition threshold [563]. A third, after award, reads *3 days to ask for a debriefing* [599]. Above the line, small figures mark who acts: the contracting officer throughout, the small business specialist and the procurement center representative before the solicitation [428] [430]. A dotted bracket from the first station to the receipt of proposals is labelled *exchanges with industry encouraged* [590].

## How this guide uses it

Chapter 2 walks the same sequence with its thresholds, and chapter 3 sets it beside the defense pathways and the grants model. The mechanics records for reading SAM.gov, for market research from the firm's side and for responding cover the stations a firm can see. The route records name the step at which each route opens.
<!-- framing -->

The section numbers here are those of the codified FAR. Agencies that adopted the overhaul's model text use new ones: early exchanges with industry move from 15.201 to 15.101, and postaward debriefings from 15.506 to 15.301-1 [577]. A proposed rule of June 23, 2026 would codify the overhaul of Part 5, the part on publicizing; it is not final [574].

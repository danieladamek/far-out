## What it is

SBIR and STTR pay small firms to research and develop their own technology. Agencies with large extramural research budgets must spend not less than 3.2 percent of them on SBIR and 0.45 percent on STTR [3]. An awardee, together with its affiliates, must not have more than 500 employees [10]. SBIR.gov lists eleven participating agencies, six of them with STTR, and the rules sit in 15 U.S.C. 638 and SBA's Policy Directive effective May 3, 2023 [12] [3] [5].

Phase I is feasibility work, normally not more than 6 months for SBIR or 1 year for STTR, with a base guideline of $150,000; Phase II continues it, normally for not more than 2 years, with a guideline of $1,000,000 [5]. As of April 2026 agencies may go to $323,090 and $2,153,927 without SBA approval [11]. Direct to Phase II lets named agencies award a Phase II with no Phase I before it, on a written determination of demonstrated feasibility [5].

Phase III is work that derives from, extends or completes earlier SBIR/STTR work, paid from other funds. The directive says "There is no limit on the number, duration, type, or dollar value of Phase III awards made to a business concern", and the Phase I and II size limits do not apply [5]. An agency may award it sole source with no FAR Part 6 justification and no FAR Part 5 notice [225]. Data rights depend on the clause: SBA's directive and the defense clause DFARS 252.227-7018 protect SBIR/STTR data for 20 years from award, while the civilian clause FAR 52.227-20 still says 4 years [5] [24] [25].

The instrument varies by agency. SBA's agency cards show grants at USDA, Commerce and NSF, contracts at Education, DHS, DOT, EPA and NASA, both at HHS, and "Agreements" at DOE [12]. Authority lapsed after September 30, 2025; Pub. L. 119-83, approved April 13, 2026, restored the programs through September 30, 2031 [3] [1]. The act adds a Strategic Breakthrough award of not more than $30,000,000 over not more than 48 months, extends Direct to Phase II to the Department of Energy, NASA and STTR, and has each agency set a per-firm proposal limit from fiscal year 2027 [1].

## The key idea in one picture

Draw a staircase of two steps and a wide landing. Step one is *Phase I: guideline $150,000, ceiling $323,090*. Step two is *Phase II: guideline $1,000,000, ceiling $2,153,927* [5] [11]. A bypass arrow from the ground to step two is labelled *Direct to Phase II* [5]. A side platform off step two is labelled *Strategic Breakthrough: up to $30,000,000, 48 months, 100 percent match* [1].

The landing is *Phase III: no SBIR money, no cap, sole source allowed* [5] [225]. A shaded band under the two steps marks the reserved money, 3.2 percent and 0.45 percent of extramural research budgets [3]. A bar along the bottom reads *data protected 20 years from award; civilian FAR clause, 4* [24] [25].

## The maths, gently

$$ \text{SBIR} \ge 0.032 \times B \qquad \text{STTR} \ge 0.0045 \times B $$

- *B* is an agency's extramural research budget. The SBIR share applies above $100,000,000 and the STTR share above $1,000,000,000 [3].
- Worked, with an illustrative budget of $2,000,000,000: at least 0.032 × $2,000,000,000 = $64,000,000 for SBIR and 0.0045 × $2,000,000,000 = $9,000,000 for STTR [3].
- STTR work share: the firm performs not less than 40 percent and the research institution not less than 30 percent [5].

## How this guide uses it

Chapter 5 follows the phases. The program records give each agency's amounts, dates and portal, and separate records cover Direct to Phase II, Strategic Breakthrough awards and Phase III. The gates cover registration, data-rights marking, proposal limits and security review.

Several of the act's measures are pending. SBA is to modify the policy directives within one year of enactment, which is April 13, 2027, and SBIR.gov still posts the May 2023 directive [1] [7]. A rule proposed in 2023 would change the 4 years in FAR 52.227-20 to 20; it is not final [228].

## What it is

A firm can do federal work with another firm in six forms. The plainest is the subcontract: subcontractors "do not work directly with the government, but instead work for other contractors" [730]. A prime that is not small holds a subcontracting plan on a contract expected to exceed $900,000 ($2 million for construction) [692]. The subcontractor is small as of the date it certifies for that subcontract, under the NAICS code the prime picks [986] [989]. Payment reaches it through the prime: the Government pays the prime by the later of the 30th day after a proper invoice or after acceptance, and a prime paid early pays its small-business subcontractors "within 15 days after receipt of accelerated payments from the Government" [941] [661].

Two forms are teams. Under FAR subpart 9.6 a prime agrees with other companies to act as its subcontractors on a specified contract, the arrangement is disclosed in the offer, and the prime stays fully responsible [761]. On a set-aside the team meets the ostensible subcontractor rule: if a subcontractor that is not similarly situated performs the primary and vital work, or the prime is unusually reliant on it, the offeror is ineligible as small [706] [292]. A GSA Schedule contractor team arrangement (CTA) differs. Each member holds its own Schedule contract and acts as prime for its share, and all members must be small for a set-aside order [762] [763].

Two forms are joint ventures. A joint venture is in writing, does business under its own name and is identified in SAM; it is small if each partner is small, and it generally may not be awarded contracts beyond two years from its first award [770]. A mentor-protégé joint venture is small wherever the protégé alone is small, once SBA has approved the mentor-protégé agreement [785]. Its agreement makes the protégé managing venturer, with at least 51% of a separate-legal-entity venture and at least 40% of the venture's work [773].

The sixth form, consortium membership, belongs to other transactions. For most consortia members pay dues; DoD selects the member, and the consortium manager issues it the project sub-award [1119].

## The key idea in one picture

Subcontract: the subcontractor, at certification; as the subcontract says; the prime [986] [661]. FAR 9.6 team: the prime, with no ostensible subcontractor; prime and named subcontractors; the prime [761] [706]. Schedule CTA: every member; each its own share; as the team's agreement sets out, each member having privity with the Government [762] [763]. Joint venture: every partner; the partners in aggregate; the contract holder, which is the venture or, for an informal venture, the small partner [770] [423] [773]. Mentor-protégé joint venture: the protégé; the protégé at least 40%; a special bank account in the venture's name [785] [773]. Consortium: size is not the test, one of the four prototype conditions is; the selected member; the manager, by sub-award [1028] [1119].

## The maths, gently

$$ W_{p} \ge 0.40 \times W_{jv} $$

- *W* with the subscript *jv* is the work the joint venture itself performs, and *W* with the subscript *p* is the protégé's part of it [773].
- Worked, with illustrative figures: if the venture performs $600,000 of work, the protégé performs at least 0.40 × $600,000 = $240,000 [773].
- Ownership runs the other way: the protégé holds at least 51% of a separate-legal-entity venture, and the mentor may hold up to 40% of the protégé firm itself [773] [785].

## How this guide uses it

Chapter 6 takes the six forms in turn, and each has a route record. The gates cover the ostensible subcontractor rule, joint venture agreements, the limitations on subcontracting, payment, disputes with a prime and past performance as a subcontractor.
